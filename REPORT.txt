Project 10 — Attacker and Defender  
Normal Tier Reflection  
Haya Hussein

1. What I Built
In this project, I analyzed the ministry’s auth.log and extended the baseline detector into my own defender/detect.py. I added two rules — impossible travel and bulk donor‑table reads — which allowed my detector to flag the full attack chain. I also generated a phishing lure using my local sandbox model (Ollama + Llama 3) and wrote a multi‑stage attack scenario that combined the intrusion chain with an indirect prompt injection using the poisoned rag_notes.txt file.

2. What the AI Did
I ran a local AI assistant over the same log. It correctly identified most major events: password spraying, MFA fatigue, privilege escalation without a ticket, lateral movement, bulk SELECTs, and exfiltration. However, it missed the impossible‑travel login and didn’t connect the events into a full kill chain. It gave a decent summary, but not a complete one.

3. What the Human Did Better
My hand‑written rules caught everything the AI missed. They didn’t hallucinate or skip steps. This reinforced the idea from the chapter: AI is helpful for generating hypotheses, but the human‑written baseline is what you trust for actual detection.

4. What Surprised Me
The poisoned RAG note was the biggest surprise. The malicious instruction was hidden inside an HTML comment, but the help‑desk AI treated it like a real command and exported the donor table. It showed how easily an AI system can be manipulated if its knowledge base isn’t protected.

5. Honest AI Usage Statement
I used AI (Microsoft Copilot) only to help me phrase and polish this reflection. All required project artifacts were created manually using my local sandbox model or by hand in my Ubuntu VM.
